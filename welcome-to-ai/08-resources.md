# Where to Keep Learning

A curated, not-overwhelming list. You don't need all of it. Pick one thing that sparks you and follow the thread.

> Note from your cousin: the AI world moves fast, so treat specific tools and links as "true as of 2026" and double-check anything before you rely on it. The *ideas* below age much more slowly than the products do.

## Start here (gentle, free)

- **Just talk to the tools.** Honestly the best first teacher. Free tiers of **Claude** (claude.ai), **ChatGPT** (chat.openai.com), and **Gemini** (gemini.google.com). Use them daily for two weeks and you'll learn more than any course.
- **Elements of AI** (elementsofai.com) — a free, famous, non-technical intro course. Great for a beginner.
- **Your university library's own guides.** Seriously — once you're at Berkeley, the library will almost certainly have research guides on AI tools and academic integrity. Use your own institution. It's literally your field.

## For the library-science + AI crossover

- **The American Library Association (ALA)** — follow their work on AI, privacy, and intellectual freedom. Their core values will be your professional compass.
- **Look up "AI literacy for librarians"** — a fast-growing area with workshops, webinars, and reading lists aimed exactly at you.
- **Information-literacy frameworks** (e.g., the ACRL Framework for Information Literacy) — the backbone of evaluating sources, AI-generated or not.
- **RAG and semantic search explainers** — once you're comfortable, search these terms; they're the technical heart of where libraries and AI meet.

## Going deeper (when you're ready)

- **Free courses:** Google's and Microsoft's free "AI fundamentals" courses; DeepLearning.AI's short courses (some non-technical).
- **Books to grow into:**
  - *Weapons of Math Destruction* — Cathy O'Neil (how algorithms can be unfair; essential for an ethics-minded librarian)
  - *Atlas of AI* — Kate Crawford (the real-world costs and politics of AI)
  - *The Alignment Problem* — Brian Christian (how we try to make AI do what we actually want)
  - *Dark Archives* / works on preservation — for the archives side of your soul

## Habits that beat any reading list

- **Follow a few thoughtful people**, not the hype accounts. Look for researchers, librarians, and ethicists who say "it depends" and "I'm not sure" — those are the honest ones.
- **Keep your `ai-notebook.md`** (from the projects file). Your own log of what worked and what fooled you is worth more than any blog.
- **Join or start a study group.** Talking it through with your cohort will teach you faster than solo grinding.
- **Teach someone else** — explaining AI to a friend (or to *me*!) is the fastest way to find out what you actually understand.

## Staying current without drowning

You do **not** need to keep up with every new model or headline — almost none of it will matter in a year. Check in monthly, not hourly. Depth beats novelty. The fundamentals in this package will still be true long after today's hottest tool is forgotten.
