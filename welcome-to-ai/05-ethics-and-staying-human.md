# Ethics & Staying Human

The technical stuff you'll pick up fast. This is the part that actually matters, and it's the part a lot of people skip. Don't skip it.

## The line that keeps you out of trouble

> **Use AI to *do* your work better. Never use it to *avoid doing* your work.**

Using AI to brainstorm a thesis, then writing the paper yourself? Great. Having AI write the paper and submitting it as yours? That's plagiarism, and it's also you robbing *yourself* of the education you earned a scholarship to get. The honest uses are the ones that leave you smarter at the end.

## Academic integrity, concretely

- **Ask first.** Each professor and each assignment may have different AI rules. Get the policy before you start. "Is AI allowed here, and for what?"
- **Disclose when unsure.** A simple note — "I used AI to brainstorm and to check my grammar" — is almost always the safe move.
- **Never submit unverified AI text as fact.** Fake citations are the classic way students get caught. Every source must be real and must actually say what you claim.
- **The work has to be yours.** If you can't explain it without the chatbot open, you haven't learned it yet.

## Things AI gets wrong that you need to watch for

- **Bias.** Models learn from human text, so they inherit human prejudice — about race, gender, culture, who counts as an "expert." As a future steward of information, noticing and naming this is part of your job.
- **Hallucination.** Confident, fluent, and wrong. Always assume facts need checking.
- **Erasure & flattening.** AI trained mostly on dominant languages and cultures can quietly sideline smaller voices, dialects, and histories. Libraries exist partly to protect those. Stay alert to what's missing.
- **A false sense of neutrality.** "The computer said so" feels objective. It isn't. Every model reflects choices made by the people who built it.

## Privacy — your field's superpower value

Libraries have one of the strongest privacy ethics of any institution on earth: what you read is nobody's business. Many AI tools are built on the opposite instinct — collect everything. You're going to live at that tension your whole career. Start the habit now:

- Don't feed private or personal data into chatbots.
- Read (okay, skim) what a tool does with what you type.
- Champion the patron's right to read and search without being tracked.

## The bigger questions you'll get to wrestle with

You don't need answers to these today. But these are the conversations your generation of librarians will lead:

- Who owns the knowledge AI was trained on? Were authors credited or paid?
- If AI helps decide what people see, who's accountable when it's wrong or unfair?
- How do we keep information free and open when powerful tools sit behind paywalls?
- How do we make sure AI *widens* access to knowledge instead of concentrating it?

These are library questions. They always were.

## Staying human

A few things no machine should take from you:

- **Your curiosity.** Keep asking your own questions, not just the ones it's easy to type.
- **Your voice.** Write like *you*. The world has enough beige AI prose.
- **Your relationships.** The best part of libraries was never the books — it was the people. Help real humans. Talk to your professors. Make friends in your cohort.
- **Your struggle.** The hard, slow, confusing part of learning *is* the learning. Don't let AI smooth it away entirely. Sit in the not-knowing sometimes. That's where the growth is.

## The whole thing, in one sentence

> Let AI make you faster, never make you smaller. Stay curious, stay honest, stay kind — and remember that the point of every tool is to help real people, which is the same reason libraries exist.
